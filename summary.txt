We take the waveform (audio signal) and divide it into small chunks (frames).
For each chunk, we compute energy by squaring and summing the values, which tells us how strong the signal is.
We also convert amplitude into dB (log scale) to better approximate how humans perceive loudness.

Then we use a spectrogram to see how energy is distributed across different frequencies over time (not just the highest frequency).

MFCCs are derived from the spectrogram by transforming it into a form that matches human hearing and compressing it into coefficients that capture the overall sound texture.

Finally, we use these extracted features to define rules that map audio characteristics to moods.
